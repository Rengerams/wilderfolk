# Wilderfolk Roadmap

<p align="center">
  <em>Where the valley goes next.</em>
</p>

**A living landscape. A settlement that feels like yours. A valley that remembers.**

Wilderfolk is a cozy frontier settlement sim built *inside* the food chain — you don't tame the wild, you
move into it. This is the plan for what's coming: smaller features you'll feel on day one, and bigger
projects that change what the valley *is*.

---

## 🏔️ A valley that behaves like a real place

This is the headline project, and most of it is already in the game.

- **Mountains that make sense.** Ranges cluster into ridgelines instead of scattering into random bumps.
  Walk over one and you'll find a **wet side and a dry side** — lush to windward, open and arid to lee.
  It's how real ranges work, and now it's how yours does.
- **Rivers that actually drain the land.** Every stream belongs to a catchment: headwaters are thin,
  the trunk river is broad, and valleys are carved by the water that runs through them. Lakes sit in
  basins. Waterfalls appear where the land drops. Rivers no longer cross each other or stop dead inland.
- **Fifteen biomes that blend instead of butting up against each other.** Forests give way to meadow,
  meadow to steppe, steppe to stone — with **no tile seams**, no checkerboard, no stair-step coastlines.
  Shorelines and lake edges are smooth curves.
- **Climate you can travel through.** The middle of the map is its warm band and the rims cool toward the
  poles, so a Nordic map reads as boreal forest and cold lakes, an Arabian one as open desert — each map
  keeps its own character, from its own climate.
- **Grit underfoot.** Stone has bedding ledges and rubble. Snow is wind-worked into drifts. Dirt shows
  gravel and pebbles. Grass has tussocks, blades and soil showing through. Zoom right in — it holds up.
- **Relief you can read.** Light and shade now carry a mountain's *shape*, not just its slope: ridges
  catch the sun, hollows fall into shade, and a valley floor sits low the way it should.
- **Bigger worlds, on the way.** The valley's largest maps are the next step — more wilderness to cross,
  more horizons to settle toward.

## 🌦️ Weather with a pulse

- **Clouds that drift with your camera**, and fog bands that hang over the valley in layers.
- **Rain, storms, drought and snow** — each with its own mood, its own light, and its own effect on the
  scene. Fog's veil finally draws the way it was always meant to.
- **A daylight warmth cast** across the day, and season particles in the air.
- **Rivers that flow and sparkle** in the sun, and **shores that shimmer** along the waterline.
- Weather moves at the same speed on every screen — no double-speed rain on fast displays.

## 🏘️ A settlement you can read at a glance

- **Contact shadows** ground every settler, animal, tree and building in the world.
- **Warm windows and chimney smoke** when your people are home for the night, **forge fire** at the
  blacksmith, and **work dust** where they're building.
- **Sprite art that holds together** — one light direction, one palette, one outline weight, one sense of
  scale across people, animals, trees and buildings. (Already upgraded in a previous version.)
- **Coming to the settlement layer:** road aprons that show where paths begin, crops growing in the
  fields, lanterns in the lanes after dark, and clearer cues for who is working where.

## 🎮 Game feel — the bits that make it a *game*, not a page

- **Gamepad support**, so you can play from the sofa.
- **True fullscreen, pointer lock and a hiding cursor** — the biggest "this is a browser tab" tell, gone.
- **A HUD made for playing**, not for reading a dashboard.
- **Deeper sound** — direction, space and warmth, on top of the existing music and effect mix.
- **Softer dawn and dusk**, restrained fog and richer atmosphere to come, once the day/night work lands
  in a way this game's fast, cozy days deserve.

## 🧭 Clarity and depth

- **A council report that plans your week**, not just your day.
- **"Why?" for everything** — not only your settlers, but your buildings, your shortages and your warnings.
- **The Operations Center grows up**: alerts that resolve, one click to the exact person or building, and
  advice that survives a reload.
- **More stories.** Festivals, village requests, ecology signals, rival drama — all written on the system
  you already have, so the valley keeps surprising you with things that actually mean something.

## ⚙️ Under the hood, calmly

Big worlds, thousands of settlers, no stutter — the valley is built to hold a **full year of play on the
largest maps** without a hiccup, and it already does. Next: make the biggest maps feel effortless, and
keep the valley's memory small however long you play.

**The valley stays cozy.** Wilderfolk is a settlement sim with teeth, not a survival trial: crisis and
disaster systems are not planned, and the weather stays scenery and challenge rather than punishment.
That's a standing call, not an oversight.
